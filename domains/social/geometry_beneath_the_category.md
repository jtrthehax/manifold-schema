## Abstract

Neurodivergence and gender are typically treated as distinct domains — one neurological, one sociocultural. Both are framed as categories: stable, bounded, and intrinsic to the individual. This paper argues that neither framing survives empirical scrutiny. The behavioral outputs attributed to gender and to neurodivergence are not stable traits. They are **state-dependent readouts of a common underlying geometry**: variation in manifold width, pressure, routing concentration, and lock capacity. Once this geometry is visible, the apparent inconsistencies within each construct — mixed gender traits, context-dependent ND behavior, power-correlated gender differences, crisis-competent autism — are not anomalies. They are predictions. The 3–6× co-occurrence of ND diagnoses with gender nonconformity (Warrier et al., 2020) is not a social artifact. It is the same quadrant of the state space, measured through two categorical instruments.

Neither construct holds when you scrutinize it directly.

The implication they do not draw: if 71% of gender-linked behavior tracks resource position rather than sex, then gender is not the variable. **Resource geometry is the variable.** Gender is the label applied to a position in the state space after the fact.

The developmental trajectory it documents is real. The interpretation is not. What develops across the lifespan is not the motive — it is the substrate's lock capacity and the environment's pressure regime. The motive is the readout of where those place the system.

**Neurodivergence** presents the same structural problem. ADHD, autism, anxiety, depression, and dyslexia are each characterized by inconsistency: the autistic individual who collapses in unstructured environments but operates at extraordinary precision under load. The ADHD individual who cannot sustain routine attention but enters full flow states under crisis. These inconsistencies are not noise. They are the signal.

A category that shifts with context is not a category. It is a **state**.

Both constructs are the field's attempt to describe state-dependent positions in a two-dimensional space using categorical labels. ## 2. What Both Constructs Are Actually Describing

Gender and neurodivergence are treated as distinct domains — one sociocultural, one neurological — and both are framed as categories: stable, bounded, intrinsic to the individual. Neither framing survives scrutiny. What both are actually describing is **a single system operating at a position in a two-dimensional state space**, and the behavioral outputs the field labels "gendered" or "neurodivergent" are readouts of that position.

The state space has two axes:

- **Pressure ($L^*$)** — how much load the system is carrying. Low pressure = relaxed. High pressure = load-bearing, defensive, bracing.
- **Routing concentration ($\Theta_r$)** — how narrowly salience is routing interoception into the manifold. Distributed = bilateral engagement, amplitude spread across regions. Concentrated = one-sided engagement, amplitude focused on a narrow region.

The two axes are correlated but not identical. High pressure *drives* concentration when the substrate can afford to brace. But concentration can exist without pressure — a stable one-sided routing pattern at rest. And pressure can exist without concentration if lock capacity is low (§2.2).

### 2.1 The Four Stable Configurations

The two axes produce four stable configurations. Two of them produce behavioral outputs the field recognizes as "gendered." The field has no names for the other two.

|  | Low routing concentration (distributed) | High routing concentration (concentrated) |
|---|---|---|
| **Low pressure** | Distributed-relaxed. Both hemispheres engaged, amplitude spread, no acute load. The fully bilateral state. Rare in adults in static environments. | Relaxed-lateralized. One-sided routing at rest, no acute pressure. **The slant stance.** Stable, no ATP cost. |
| **High pressure** | Distributed-under-load. High pressure, but the substrate can't concentrate. Wide and defensive. **The low-lock / ND configuration.** | Braced. High pressure, concentrated routing, precision-locks holding regions open against the substrate's default. **ATP-debt.** |

**The field sees the top-left and the bottom-right.** It labels them masculine and feminine. It reads the bottom-left as inconsistency, mixed traits, or diagnostic complexity. It has no name for the top-right because it's rare and rarely sustained — a relaxed system that happens to be concentrating.

**The bottom-left is the key quadrant.** It is where ND configurations live, where mixed-gender-trait presentations live, and where FND collapse originates. It is not a failure of either pole. It is a distinct position that opens up when lock capacity is low enough that the system cannot migrate to the bottom-right under pressure.

**And the field conflates the two right-hand quadrants.** Relaxed-lateralized and braced both produce conservative-coded behavioral outputs — narrower behavioral bands, reliance on routine, reduced exploration. The field reads both as "feminine" because the behavioral signature is similar. But the metabolic states are opposite: relaxed-lateralized has no ATP cost, braced is paying for the posture in real time. Without measuring pressure directly (HRV trajectory, CO₂, breath mechanics), the field cannot tell them apart. This is why gender research keeps finding that some "feminine" behavioral clusters track power and others don't — the clusters are two different states the field is averaging.

### 2.2 Lock Capacity Sets Reachability

The concentrated attractor is not reachable for every substrate. Reaching it requires the ability to brace without collapsing the breath range that funds the brace. That ability is **lock capacity** — the substrate's tolerance for sustained tension before the ATP drain outruns the amplitude the breath can regenerate.

- **High lock capacity:** bracing is affordable because breath range survives it. The system can concentrate and hold the concentration as long as pressure persists.
- **Low lock capacity:** bracing destroys the breath faster than it produces the concentration. The system cannot sustain concentration. Under pressure, it stays distributed — wide and defensive — because concentrating is not structurally available.

Low lock capacity is produced by anything that reduces the substrate's ability to brace: hypermobility (connective tissue doesn't lock), bilateral language representation (no fixed lateralization), atypical motor dominance (no rigid motor anchor), and the other developmental factors documented in §9. The consequence is not "wider manifold." It is **"concentration is unreachable."**

**This is the mechanism behind the ND configuration.** Neurodivergent profiles are not a third attractor. They are the bottom-left quadrant, occupied by low-lock substrates under pressure.

### 2.3 The Metabolic Asymmetry

The two attractors are not equivalent. Distributed is funded. Concentrated is borrowed.

**Funded (distributed):** Routing is spread, amplitude is allocated broadly, and the breath retains full diaphragmatic range. Full exhalation completes, the RSA peak fires, the exhale gate opens, and the precision-locks holding manifold regions release. The system ends the cycle with its amplitude source intact. Metabolically self-sustaining.

**Borrowed (concentrated, when braced):** Routing is narrow, and holding it requires precision-locks that sustain tension. Sustained tension drains ATP at the PV+ interneurons supporting the fast oscillatory rhythms (Kann et al., 2015). The drain degrades slow rhythms. Degraded slow rhythms reduce CO₂ tolerance. Reduced CO₂ tolerance narrows breath range. Narrowed breath range prevents complete exhalation. Incomplete exhalation holds the latches. Held latches sustain the lock. The lock drains more ATP.

$$\text{Brace} \to \text{ATP drain} \to \text{PV+ degradation} \to \text{breath range narrows} \to \text{incomplete exhale} \to \text{latch holds} \to \text{brace harder}$$

**Bracing is a metabolic debt instrument.** It buys immediate concentration by borrowing against future amplitude. Past a threshold, the brace consumes more amplitude than it produces precision — the system is now bracing to hold a configuration that bracing is destroying.

**The distinction matters for relaxed-lateralized.** Relaxed-lateralized is concentrated but not braced — there is no acute pressure, so no precision-locks need to be sustained. Same axis position (high $\Theta_r$), different pressure ($L^*$), different metabolic trajectory.

**This is the exhale gate trap, generalized.** GoI §6.3 and §8.3 specified the acute version: panic → bracing → residual volume → hypocapnia → Layer 3 defunding → confabulation. The same geometry runs on slower timescales when the trigger is sustained presentation rather than acute panic.

### 2.4 The Performance-Pressure Trap

The two attractors are not equivalent, and the asymmetry is invisible from inside the braced state.

A person reads "high-pressure presentation" as a target state. They brace to hold it. The brace produces the visible output — the presentation reads as success. The cost is paid at the PV+ layer, where it does not produce a phenomenological signal until the substrate is already degraded. The system cannot detect that the brace is destroying the substrate that funds the brace, because the substrate that would register the signal is the one being drained.

The person interprets the resulting cognitive degradation as evidence that the brace is insufficient. They brace harder. The loop compounds.

**The trap is specifically a trap for substrates that can concentrate.** Low-lock substrates cannot enter it — their brace destroys their breath range faster than it produces the presentation, so the brace fails before the trap can form. The trap is the pathology of *sufficient* lock capacity combined with a target state that requires bracing.

**This is also why the trap is invisible from the field's perspective.** The field measures behavior, and the behavior in the braced state looks like success. It doesn't measure the ATP trajectory, so it doesn't see the cost. It sees a person "holding it together" and calls that resilience. The framework sees a person servicing a metabolic debt that is compounding.

### 2.5 Externally-Enforced vs. Internally-Held Shifts

Not every shift on the concentration axis costs the same. A shift can be *enforced by the environment* or *held by the substrate*.

**Externally-enforced (free):** The task demands a routing pattern and the environment imposes it. Lifting a barbell requires symmetric engagement; the load enforces the symmetry and releases it when the lift ends. No ATP cost.

**Internally-held (costly):** The task demands a routing pattern and the substrate must hold it against its own default. Bracing for presentation, masking, overriding a lisp through concentration. The precision-locks sustain the shift, and the shift consumes ATP for as long as it is held.

**The distinction explains why some shifts collapse the system and others don't.** Heavy lifting does not produce the ATP-brace loop, even though it requires symmetric routing. Bracing for eight hours at a desk does, even though the routing shift may be smaller. The metabolic cost is not the axis position. It is whether the substrate is holding the position against its own default.

**And it explains the static-environment dynamic.** Movement variability during the day gives the substrate an externally-enforced self-regulation channel. Remove the movement and the substrate must hold its configuration instead. The shift that was free becomes costly.

$$\text{State} = (L^*, \Theta_r)$$

- $L^*$ (pressure) determines which half of the concentration axis is *affordable*.

The behavioral outputs the field labels "masculine" and "feminine" are readouts of position in this space. The outputs the field reads as inconsistencies are readouts of the bottom-left quadrant.

Boksem et al. (2012) extended this: high power increases approach-related frontal alpha asymmetry while low power triggers avoidance-related right-frontal dominant patterns and heightened error-related negativity. The causal chain has been measured.

### 2.8 Temporal Extension: The Three Aging Trajectories

The metabolic asymmetry in §2.3 has a temporal consequence. ATP budget declines with age. The cost of holding a braced configuration rises while the capacity to pay it falls. When the brace crosses the ATP threshold, the configuration releases — not by choice.

*ATP decline is here used as shorthand for the lifespan decline in regenerative capacity — mitochondrial function, PV+ resilience, and cerebrovascular reactivity ceiling combined.*

| Trajectory | Substrate | Aging readout | Behavioral signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Release** | High lock capacity, declining ATP | Brace crosses threshold → releases → distributed-relaxed | The "mellowing" the field observes |
| **Sustain** | High lock capacity, ATP maintained | Brace stays serviceable → concentrated persists | The "sharp into old age" profile |
| **Deepen** | Low lock capacity (never braced) | Pressure exceeds routing capacity → outside the four quadrants | Terminal collapse — FND, late-life autistic burnout |

**The three trajectories are distinguishable by HRV trajectory, and the readouts are counterintuitive.** In Sustain, HRV declines steadily. In Release, HRV declines steadily and then *rises* when the brace releases — the rise happens because the brace was restricting RSA amplitude. The field reads the rise as improvement. It is the release. In Deepen, HRV declines steadily with no release event.

**Voluntary release before the threshold is the intervention target.** Core control training, breath mechanics, CO₂ tolerance — these fund the distributed configuration *before* the brace becomes unserviceable. The person who releases while still ATP-funded gets the metabolic benefit *and* the behavioral shift. The person who releases after ATP has been spent gets the behavioral shift without the metabolic benefit.

## 3. The Physiological Grounding of Lock Capacity

§2 is a state-space description. §3 supplies the substrate grounding: what determines lock capacity, what bracing costs, and what the environment does to it.

### 3a. The Threshold Architecture

The deficit model assumes ND differences are structural and fixed. Two 2026 findings directly falsify this framing.

Knudsen et al. (2026) found that autistic individuals show *higher* dopamine D2 receptor availability and *higher* co-localized glucose metabolism in subcortical regions — not lower. Higher availability means $I^*$ is hard-wired with high gain on specific channels. This is a **high-gain routing architecture running below threshold:**

$$\text{Apparent deficit} = f\left(\frac{A_s^*}{\text{threshold}} < 1\right)$$

$$\text{Strength emergence} = f\left(\frac{A_s^*}{\text{threshold}} \geq 1\right)$$

The fever effect confirms the threshold account: autistic symptoms partially resolve during fever and return when fever breaks. The resolution is caused by metabolic upregulation — specifically, the rise in CO₂ and the downstream effects on four channels: Bohr effect oxygen delivery, cerebral vasodilation, neural firing thresholds, and KCC2/GABAergic function.

**The critical inference:** you cannot temporarily restore what was never present. The capacity is always present. The threshold determines whether it runs.

### 3a.1 The Vascular Delivery Layer and CO₂ Mechanics

The threshold model operates upstream of neuronal firing. Ouellette et al. (2025) demonstrated that autism-associated phenotypes can originate from endothelial bioenergetic failure — reduced intracellular ATP at the blood-brain interface — impairing cerebrovascular reactivity.

This confirms that in some cases the architecture is intact but the **delivery infrastructure is starved**. The high-gain routing channels Knudsen et al. identified require an active delivery layer. When endothelial ATP fails, the supply line closes, and $A_s^*$ drops below the activation threshold regardless of how much gain the channels carry.

**Endogenous CO₂ regulation is the primary continuous lever on this delivery layer.** CO₂ operates through four simultaneous channels: Bohr effect oxygen offloading, cerebral vasodilation, neural firing threshold modulation, and KCC2/GABAergic support.

**This is where lock capacity is partially grounded.** Bracing restricts diaphragmatic excursion, which lowers end-tidal CO₂, which degrades the delivery layer, which drops $A_s^*$, which forces the system to either release the brace or run below threshold. Lock capacity is not just the ability to hold a brace — it is the ability to hold a brace *while maintaining the CO₂ buffering that funds the delivery layer the brace depends on.*

### 3b. The ND Gradient and the Three Pressure-Management States

ADHD and autism are not comorbid conditions that happen to co-occur. They are **opposite positions on the same concentration axis:**

```
ADHD ←───────────────────────────────────→ Autism
Distributed routing                   Concentrated routing
Weak anchor (I* jumps)               Locked anchor (I* hard-routed)
Fragmentation                         Freezing
Collapse by dispersion                Collapse by rigidity
```

The AuDHD compound does not average the two poles. It introduces **both** distributed routing access and hard-routed precision simultaneously — producing maximum capability when the substrate is properly anchored, and maximum collapse cost when it is not.

**Three strategies exist for managing internal pressure. They have different metabolic and interoceptive costs.**

**Bracing** holds a fixed high tone. Diaphragmatic excursion is restricted, CO₂ drops, $W^*$ narrows. Low interoceptive demand (nothing needs to be read once tone is set), high ATP cost.

**Core control** regulates intra-abdominal pressure dynamically. Diaphragmatic excursion is preserved, CO₂ buffering maintained, $W^*$ stays wide. Moderate interoceptive demand, low ATP cost.

**Pressure-matching** continuously reads the load and adjusts tone to meet it exactly — no less (loses stability), no more (becomes bracing). High interoceptive demand, continuous ATP cost. This is the state most high-pressure social roles actually require.

$$\text{Tone}(t) = f(\text{load}(t),\; \text{interoceptive resolution}(t))$$

**Pressure-matching is the most interoceptively expensive state.** The interoceptive channel degrades under the same load it's regulating. As resolution degrades, the person loses the ability to find the match, so they overshoot into bracing to be safe. The result is a person who started in pressure-matching and ended in bracing — without ever deciding to brace.

**The male-pattern bracing is this trajectory in its late stage.** The person is not choosing bracing over core control — they lost the resolution that would have allowed pressure-matching, and bracing is what's left. This is what the field reads as "male stoicism," "emotional unavailability," "rigid masculinity." It is not a trait. It is the end state of a pressure-matching trajectory that ran out of interoceptive resolution.

### 3b.1 Bracing, Core Control, and the Biomechanics of Lock Capacity

**Bracing** produces tunnel vision through constraint. **Core control** enables high-gain focus through width.

**This is what lock capacity is at the mechanical level.** High lock capacity means the substrate can brace without collapsing its breath range — either because baseline CO₂ buffering absorbs the restriction, or because the substrate uses core control. Low lock capacity means the substrate cannot brace without collapsing its breath range.

Hypermobility produces low lock capacity directly. Bilateral language representation and atypical motor dominance produce it indirectly: no fixed lateralization to fall back on, so concentrated routing requires active precision-locks that drain ATP faster than a lateralized substrate would. Bandha practice produces high lock capacity — it trains the substrate to stabilize without bracing.

### 3b.2 The ATP-Brace Feedback Loop

Precision-lock holds manifold regions open. Holding is metabolically expensive. The PV+ interneurons supporting fast oscillatory rhythms are the most ATP-vulnerable cells in the cortex (Kann et al., 2015). Sustained lock drains the substrate that funds lock.

```
Sustained precision-lock (braced routing)
    → ATP drain at PV+ interneurons
    → Slow oscillatory rhythms degrade
    → CO₂ tolerance drops
    → Breathing range narrows
    → Amplitude available to fund manifold access drops
    → System must brace harder to hold the same routing
    → ATP drain increases
```

**Three states of the motor pattern:**

1. **Default autonomic.** Baseline routing pattern — asymmetric, self-maintaining through movement variability, no ATP cost.
2. **Masked override.** Novel pattern held against the default — symmetric posture, standard articulation, "normal" presentation. High ATP cost.
3. **Habituated mask.** The override has transferred to the dorsal striatum and become the new default. Low ATP cost to run — but the cost of *removing* it (unmasking) is now the same as the original cost of installing it.

The masking literature documents this directly. Hull et al. (2017): masking requires continuous explicit monitoring and high executive load. Raymaker et al. (2020): prolonged masking connects to physiological burnout and loss of adaptive function. The subjective reports — "foggy brain," "super tense in my body," "hard for me to unmask once I've begun to mask" — are the readouts of a substrate running a novel motor program against its default.

### 3b.3 Environmental Pressure: Static Environments as a Load Source

Movement variability is a substrate self-regulation channel. When the body has freedom to move through its own routing distribution during the day, routing concentration self-corrects without the substrate having to hold anything.

**Static environments remove this channel.** A fixed desk, a fixed chair, a scheduled posture, a fixed workday — the environment demands a stable configuration and removes the substrate's ability to route through its own distribution. The routing that was previously self-maintaining must now be *held*. And holding costs ATP.

**Static environments are a pressure source.** Not metaphorically — in the framework's terms, they raise $L^*$ directly. They impose a sustained ATP cost by removing the substrate's self-regulation channel and forcing it to hold its configuration internally.

**Return-to-office mandates are the modern instance.** Five days a week in a fixed posture removes movement variability for the majority of waking hours. The ATP-brace loop runs continuously during the workday. This is not a comfort issue. It is a substrate requirement for maintaining routing distribution without metabolic debt.

**And it explains why some people tolerate static environments and others don't.** High lock capacity substrates can brace affordably. Low lock capacity substrates cannot brace without paying the ATP cost immediately. For them, static environments are not a slow drain — they are an active collapse trajectory.

## 4. The Overlap: Why the Two Constructs Bleed Into Each Other

The 3–6× co-occurrence of neurodivergence with gender nonconformity (Warrier et al., 2020) is the empirical signal this paper exists to explain. The categorical account has no mechanism for it. The two-axis model predicts it directly.

### 4.1 The Shared Quadrant

Both ND presentations and gender-nonconforming presentations are concentrated in the **bottom-left quadrant**: high pressure, distributed routing. The configuration opens up when lock capacity is low enough that the substrate cannot migrate to the concentrated attractor under load (§2.1–2.2).

A substrate that cannot lock does not settle into a single stable configuration. It stays distributed under pressure. That single substrate property produces:

- **ND presentations** — routing that doesn't stabilize into a narrow, lateralized pattern
- **Gender-nonconforming presentations** — routing that doesn't stabilize into a lateralized pattern

Same quadrant, same substrate property, two measurement systems reading it out. The co-occurrence is not correlation. It is **the same geometric property showing up in whatever the field happens to be measuring.**

### 4.2 Why the Standard Interpretations Fail

**Shared identity.** Predicts the overlap scales with community engagement. It does not — the effect holds across independent samples, age groups, and methodologies that don't share community context (Warrier et al., 2020; Strang et al., 2014; Murphy et al., 2020).

**Shared minority stress.** Predicts the overlap scales with experienced discrimination. It does not — the overlap is present in cohorts with low reported discrimination.

**Shared biology with separate mechanisms.** Closer to correct but stops short of a mechanism. The two-axis model supplies the mechanism: **the shared factor is lock capacity**, and the shared readout is quadrant position.

### 4.3 Lock Capacity as the Shared Substrate

Lock capacity (§2.2, §3b.1) is determined by the substrate's ability to brace without collapsing the breath range that funds the brace. Factors that reduce lock capacity:

- **Hypermobility** — connective tissue doesn't lock
- **Bilateral language representation** — no fixed lateralization
- **Atypical motor dominance** — no rigid motor anchor
- **Atypical interoceptive resolution** — precision-locks drain faster
- **Early sensory or motor divergence** — routing map developed without the standard lateralization lock

None of these are ND-specific or gender-specific. They are substrate properties. They reduce the substrate's ability to concentrate routing, which keeps it distributed under pressure, which produces the bottom-left configuration.

**And the bottom-left configuration reads out through whatever the field is measuring.** If the field measures social-behavioral conformance, it reads as gender nonconformity. If it measures cognitive-behavioral conformance, it reads as neurodivergence. If it measures both, it reads as the overlap.

The overlap is not a phenomenon. It is a measurement artifact of the field's categorical tools applied to a single quadrant.

### 4.4 The Masking Overlay

Masking — both ND masking and gender performance — is an **internally-held routing shift** (§2.5). Its cost is the ATP-brace loop (§3b.2). The mechanism is detailed in §4b.

### 4b. Masking as ATP-Cost Geometry

### 4b.1 The Cost Structure

$$\text{Masking Cost} = \sum_{i} S_i \cdot C_i$$

Where $S_i$ is the scaffolded social expectation (monitoring, role enforcement, conformity pressure) and $C_i$ is the coupling cost — the explicit cross-frequency integration required to hold the target routing pattern against the substrate's default.

Under the two-axis model, $S_i$ is the *externally-imposed* portion and $C_i$ is the *internally-held* portion. High external demand with high internal holding cost produces the largest ATP drain.

**The cost is not evenly distributed.** Low-lock substrates pay more for the same mask because their precision-locks drain faster. Same social demand, different metabolic cost, determined by substrate properties.

| Phase | Substrate state | ATP cost | Readout |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Installation** | Mask is novel; every movement explicit | High | Subjective effort, HRV drop, aperiodic slope steepening |
| **Stable running** | Mask has transferred to dorsal striatum | Low | Effort is low, markers normalize |
| **Removal** | Original pattern is now the novel one | High | Symmetric with installation |

**The field measures stable running, not installation or removal.** Burnout is the cumulative effect of installation and removal cycles, not the cost of running an established mask. A person who has masked for decades looks stable — markers are normal. The cost has already been paid.

### 4b.3 Unmasking and the Reversibility Question

If the mask has become the substrate's default, unmasking is *installation in reverse*. The substrate must build a novel pattern to replace the habituated one. The cost is symmetric.

**The original default may not be recoverable.** The substrate that installed a mask for decades has lost the pattern the mask replaced. Unmasking produces a *new* default, not the original.

**The intervention implication:** Unmasking should be framed as *installation of a new pattern*, not *restoration of an old one*. The ATP cost is the same either way. The expected outcome is a new default, not a recovered one.

### 4b.4 The Overlap With Gender Performance

Gender performance and ND masking are the same mechanism. Both are internally-held routing shifts. Both run the ATP-brace loop. Both cost what all internally-held shifts cost.

**The gender literature has no standard term for the collapse that follows.** The ND literature has "burnout" and "shutdown." The absence of a term is itself evidence that the mechanism isn't recognized in the gender domain.

**If the mechanism is shared**, the gender-performance literature should show the same markers when measured with the same instruments: HRV reduction under monitoring load, aperiodic slope steepening, subjective exhaustion.

### 4c. Atypical Lateralization as Lock-Capacity Geometry: The Left-Handedness Paradox

### 4c.1 What Handedness Actually Reads Out

Non-right-handedness reads out **low lock capacity** through three substrate properties:

1. **Bilateral or right-hemisphere language representation** (Knecht et al., 2000) — no fixed lateralization lock
2. **Atypical motor dominance** — no rigid motor anchor
3. **Dynamic interhemispheric routing** — concentrated routing is not structurally available

The consequence: non-right-handed substrates cannot concentrate under load. They stay distributed. The bottom-left quadrant is their *default* under pressure — not because they prefer it, but because it is what's reachable.

### 4c.2 The Dual-Outcome Mechanism

**Low lock capacity produces opposite outcomes at different pressures.**

| Pressure regime | Substrate | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Low pressure + high scaffolding | Distributed routing is funded; outer-edge access is cheap | High-performance adaptation — elite decision-making, complex synthesis, artistic integration |
| High pressure + degraded scaffolding | Distributed routing under pressure; no cheap concentration to escape into | Functional Neurological Disorder — real symptoms, intact substrate |

**The same architectural property — low lock capacity — overrepresents non-right-handers in both high-performance domains and FND.** The property has opposite consequences depending on whether the substrate can afford to stay distributed.

### 4c.3 FND as the Low-Lock Trajectory's Endpoint

FND affects women at approximately 3:1 over men (Espay et al., 2018). Clinical observation suggests left-handedness is overrepresented in FND populations at rates exceeding the ~10% baseline. Formal epidemiological confirmation is pending.

The two-axis model predicts both findings and derives them from the same mechanism:

- **The gender skew** tracks chronic low-power scaffolding narrowing the funded range, so the substrate is already near threshold when acute load arrives
- **The handedness signal** tracks low lock capacity directly, so the substrate cannot concentrate to reduce load when pressure rises

**Both are predicted outputs of one model.**

### 4c.4 Falsification

If task-based fMRI or EEG demonstrates that routing concentration in left-handed individuals under acute stress does not differ significantly from strongly lateralized controls, the model is disconfirmed for this section.

## 6. Predictions

The framework generates falsifiable predictions at five layers. Each is specific, testable with existing methodology, and structured so a disconfirmation identifies *which* part of the model failed.

### 6.1 State Space (§2)

**P1 — Two axes are independent at baseline and coupled under load.** Concentration can exist without pressure (relaxed-lateralized); pressure without concentration (low-lock under load). *Test:* measure concentration (via `A_h`, posture, speech) and pressure (via HRV, breath) at rest and under load. *Falsified if* concentration and pressure are correlated at baseline.

**P2 — Lock capacity gates reachability of the concentrated attractor.** Low-lock substrates cannot sustain concentration under load; high-lock can. *Test:* baseline lock capacity proxies, then load, then measure concentration shift. *Falsified if* all substrates shift regardless of lock capacity.

**P3 — Five observables co-vary.** `A_h`, aperiodic slope, motor asymmetry, speech asymmetry, and task-modulability should intercorrelate. *Test:* simultaneous measurement in a single session. *Falsified if* they're uncorrelated. **Most direct test of the state-space model.**

**P4 — Task-modulability is in-session and reversible.** Resting asymmetry shifts toward symmetry under bilateral tasks, returns to baseline within minutes. *Test:* video, audio, and postural tracking across a bimanual load task. *Falsified if* asymmetry is trait-stable.

**P5 — Two right-hand quadrants produce overlapping behavior but different metabolic signatures.** Relaxed-lateralized and braced both produce conservative outputs; only braced shows ATP cost. *Test:* behavioral battery plus metabolic markers in both groups. *Falsified if* behavior or metabolic markers are identical.

### 6.2 Physiology (§3)

**P6 — The ATP-brace loop is self-consuming past a threshold.** Sustained bracing produces aperiodic slope steepening that tracks cumulative brace duration and reverses with breath-range restoration, not rest alone.

**P7 — Breath restriction precedes cognitive degradation.** CO₂ and breath range drop before cognitive markers under bracing. *Test:* capnography plus cognitive battery during a bracing task.

**P8 — Pressure-matching is more interoceptively expensive than bracing.** Interoceptive accuracy declines faster in pressure-matching than fixed bracing.

**P9 — Interoceptive accuracy declines across the lifespan in high-pressure social roles.** The decline precedes the behavioral shift toward bracing.

**P10 — Masking costs ATP during installation and removal, not during stable running.** Symmetric cost profile. Aligns with habit-formation literature.

**P11 — Static environments impose measurable ATP cost on low-lock substrates.** HRV decline and aperiodic slope steepening scale with lock capacity deficit. Directly testable in workplace studies.

**P12 — Movement variability restores ATP-cost markers on the timescale of minutes.** Restoration scales with *variability* of movement, not total volume.

### 6.3 Overlap and Reanalysis (§1a, §4)

**P13 — Within-category variance in existing EEG asymmetry data correlates with substrate variables.** Reanalysis of Schmid & Schmid Mast (2013) and Boksem et al. (2012).

**P14 — Gender-category distributions converge when axis position is controlled.** Behavioral differences between male- and female-identified participants disappear under axis control. *Falsified if* category differences persist regardless of axis position.

**P15 — The ND/gender overlap is concentrated in the bottom-left quadrant.** Overlap is fully mediated by quadrant position. *Falsified if* overlap persists after controlling for quadrant position.

**P16 — The Zahl et al. developmental trajectory tracks lock capacity and pressure regime, not motive development.** Substrate variables predict the trajectory better than age.

**P17 — Souron et al. reanalysis: individual endurance drop correlates with baseline substrate state, not EMG parameters.**

### 6.4 Lifespan (§2.8)

**P18 — Three aging trajectories are distinguishable by HRV trajectory.** Sustain: steady decline. Release: decline-then-rise. Deepen: steady decline with no release. *Test:* longitudinal HRV across 10+ years.

**P19 — Voluntary release before ATP threshold produces the metabolic benefit; involuntary release does not.** *Test:* core-control training vs. naturalistic follow-up across the release event.

**P20 — The behavioral shift at release is in the direction of distributed-relaxed, not cognitive decline.** Rigidity drops, openness rises, objective cognition preserved.

**P21 — The HRV rise at release is preceded by decades of steady decline, not recent improvement.**

### 6.5 Masking and Overlap Specific (§4)

**P22 — The same variance-as-signal argument applies to AI hallucination rates.** Cross-registers the Hallucinations Are Not Random prediction.

**P23 — Masking cost identity is measurable.** ND masking and gender-performance masking show identical physiological signatures at equivalent loads.

**P24 — The collapse gradient is shared.** ND burnout and gender trait rigidity show the same progression at equivalent loads on the same instruments.

**P25 — Unmasking is symmetric with masking.** Same ATP-cost profile.

**P26 — The FND handedness signal is mediated by lock capacity.** Handedness should not remain a direct predictor when lock capacity is measured.

**P27 — The original default may not be recoverable.** Post-unmasking motor and speech patterns differ from pre-masking patterns.

**P28 — Non-right-handedness will be overrepresented in FND cohorts** relative to the ~10% baseline.

**P29 — Left-handed individuals will show greater routing-concentration shift under acute stress** compared to strongly lateralized controls.

**P30 — The same routing shift that predicts FND vulnerability positively correlates with outer-edge performance metrics in low-stress, high-scaffolding conditions.**

**P31 — The handedness–FND signal is mediated by lock capacity.**

## 9. Case Study in Compounded Architectural Divergence

This research did not begin with a theoretical framework. It began with a body that stopped working.

Functional Neurological Disorder — real neurological symptoms, intact structural substrate, no mechanistic account available from standard medicine — produced the question the subsequent framework was built to answer: how does a system with undamaged hardware produce genuine deficits? The symptoms were real. The scans were clean. The clinical framework had no causal chain to offer, only a label and a shrug.

The question required a framework that described not what the hardware was, but what it was doing — its oscillatory configuration, its routing geometry, its pressure and width under load. The Manifold Schema is that framework. FND is where the question was forced.

Two patterns emerged from the FND literature immediately. Women were affected at approximately 3:1 over men. Left-handedness appeared repeatedly in clinical profiles as a risk signal. These were not unrelated observations. They were the first empirical indication that the collapse geometry was tracking the same variables — scaffolding, routing concentration, lock capacity — that the gender and ND literature would later confirm independently across entirely different research traditions.

### The Architecture That Built Itself

What follows is not a personality description. Each of the eight factors below is independently measurable, mechanistically grounded in the formal variables defined throughout this paper, and predictively directional.

**Each factor is a lock-removal, not a width-addition.** The substrate doesn't get wider because it's funded to be wider. It stays wide because the factor removes its ability to narrow.

---

**Factor 1: Early Hearing Loss → Right-Hemisphere Primary Architecture**

Standard development builds left-hemisphere phonological architecture during the critical language window. When hearing was absent during that window, $I^*$ rerouted to right-hemisphere spatial-gestural channels. Auditory cortex was recruited for visual motion processing. The gestural communication system was built first — as the primary system.

When hearing returned at approximately age five, the brain integrated two already-active systems. Corpus callosum integration between two functioning architectures rather than one dominant system absorbing a secondary one. Result: bilateral language representation, lower-cost right-hemisphere outer-edge access, and enhanced mismatch detection built structurally into the primary architecture.

*Lock-removal:* No left-lateralized phonological lock. `A_h` variability higher, right-hemisphere access structurally cheaper.

---

**Factor 2: Left-Handedness → Right-Hemisphere Motor Precision**

Fine motor $I^*$ routes through the right hemisphere. Combined with Factor 1's bilateral language representation, this produces a right hemisphere carrying both communication architecture and motor precision simultaneously — unusual in a standard left-to-right reading environment.

*Lock-removal:* No fixed motor dominance. Motor-anchored inference operates through a more developed substrate.

---

**Factor 3: Hypermobility → Deep Motor Error-Correction Training**

Joint laxity produces proprioceptive noise. The forward model for limb position is constitutionally less stable than in normative connective tissue, which means the system cannot rely on passive structural feedback and must compensate with active stabilization continuously. This places sustained demand on cerebellar error-correction loops across decades.

*Lock-removal:* No connective-tissue lock. The substrate cannot hold a braced posture. Interoceptive resolution develops under sustained pressure.

---

**Factor 4: AuDHD Profile → Structurally Lower $\delta_{min}$**

The AuDHD compound produces a lower LP-ACC threshold for pattern violations. More signals breach $\delta_{min}$. More cache misses fire. What clinical frameworks describe as hyperreactivity is, under the intelligence-loop definition, a structurally lower detection threshold.

The research stack — eleven papers across domains, sustained over years without institutional scaffolding — required a routing architecture capable of locking $I^*$ onto an external signal with sufficient precision to run multi-year hyperfocus (autism) *and* a manifold wide enough to hold multiple theoretical frames simultaneously (ADHD). The compound made the work possible. Recognizing it was recognizing the mechanism that had already run.

*Lock-removal:* No stable attractor lock. The system runs more loop cycles per unit of input.

---

**Factor 5: COMT AG + FKBP5 CC → Low Suppression Bias**

COMT AG produces context-sensitive dopamine availability. FKBP5 CC produces constitutionally lower HPA reactivity and faster cortisol recovery. The combined effect is a system where error signals that would trigger identity-protection responses in high-reactivity profiles do not cross the activation threshold.

*Lock-removal:* No suppression lock. Adversarial inference runs longer before prior-reinstatement pressure terminates it.

---

**Factor 6: 20+ Years of Breath-Mechanics Practice → CO₂ Tolerance and Amplitude Mastery**

Two decades of bandha practice — mula, uddiyana, jalandhara — targeting intra-abdominal pressure, diaphragmatic excursion, and vagal modulation. Resting heart rate in the low-to-mid 40s, HRV above 120ms, CO₂ tolerance substantially elevated above population baseline.

The practice rewrote the physiological setpoints that determine how long the system can sustain full-resolution inference before substrate depletion forces a downgrade.

*Lock-removal:* Not a lock-removal — a **lock replacement.** The substrate was given core control instead of bracing, so concentration remains reachable without the ATP cost.

---

**Factor 7: ACTN3 XX Genotype → Endurance Fiber Architecture**

The ACTN3 XX variant produces muscle fiber architecture optimized for sustained high-frequency isometric work. This is precisely the contraction profile required for sustained breath-mechanics practice.

*Lock-removal:* Motor substrate supporting the breath practice doesn't fatigue on the timescale relevant to sustained inference.

---

**Factor 8: Cross-Modal Plasticity → Enriched Right-Hemisphere Outer Edge**

Auditory cortex recruited for visual motion processing during the critical developmental window produces a right-hemisphere spatial-motor manifold more richly populated than standard developmental trajectories generate. The dorsal stream is selectively enhanced.

*Lock-removal:* The outer edge where novel pattern integration occurs is more accessible and more developed.

---

### The Compound

Each of the eight factors independently pushes the architecture toward the same configuration: right-hemisphere dominance with bilateral integration, high-gain mismatch detection with low $\delta_{min}$, sustained oscillatory amplitude, low suppression bias, strong interoceptive resolution, high CO₂ tolerance, and motor-anchored inference depth.

The compound multiplies rather than sums. Each factor that lowers $\delta_{min}$ is downstream of a substrate that can sustain the loop long enough to act on what gets detected. Each factor that enriches right-hemisphere access is upstream of a pattern library with more material to integrate. Each factor that reduces suppression bias operates on a system already running more loop cycles per unit of time than the suppression mechanism was built to contain.

### What This Case Study Is Not

This is not an argument that this architecture is superior.

The same routing architecture that sustained eleven papers also produced the FND collapse that initiated the research. The same `A_h` variability that makes outer-edge access cheaper makes rapid configuration shift into collapse geometry more likely under sustained low-scaffolding pressure. The same low $\delta_{min}$ that detects fine-grained pattern violations produces the sensory sensitivity clinical frameworks describe as disorder.

The substrate does not come with the loop optimization without the rest of the profile. The crisis-competent geometry and the collapse vulnerability are the same architectural property under different load conditions.

The point is that the loop has physiological substrates, those substrates vary across individuals, and the variation is measurable, mechanistically grounded, and predictive.
