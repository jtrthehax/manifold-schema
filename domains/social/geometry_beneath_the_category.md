## Abstract

Neurodivergence and gender are typically treated as distinct domains — one neurological, one sociocultural. Both are framed as categories: stable, bounded, and intrinsic to the individual. This paper argues that neither framing survives empirical scrutiny. The behavioral outputs attributed to gender and to neurodivergence are not stable traits. They are **state-dependent oscillatory outputs** produced by a common underlying geometry: variation in manifold width, pressure, hemispheric recruitment, coupling, jitter, and scaffolding. Once this geometry is visible, the apparent inconsistencies within each construct — mixed gender traits, context-dependent ND behavior, power-correlated gender differences, crisis-competent autism — are not anomalies. They are predictions. The 3–6× co-occurrence of ND diagnoses with gender nonconformity (Warrier et al., 2020) is not a social artifact. It is a geometric necessity.

## 1. The Problem With Both Constructs

Neither construct holds when you scrutinize it directly.

**Gender** presents the simpler case. A recent large-scale meta-analysis compared 102 meta-analytic sex/gender differences against 59 experimental power manipulations and found that 71% of gender differences are consistent with power differences, producing a 9:1 ratio of consistent-to-inconsistent effects. The authors note that "brains and hormonal systems are not strictly divided into two distinct categories." They stop there.

The implication they do not draw: if 71% of gender-linked behavior tracks resource position rather than sex, then gender is not the variable. **Resource geometry is the variable.** Gender is the label applied to a particular attractor state after the fact.

**Neurodivergence** presents a more complex case, but the same structural problem. ADHD, autism, anxiety, depression, and dyslexia are each characterized by inconsistency: the autistic individual who collapses in unstructured environments but operates at extraordinary precision under load. The ADHD individual who cannot sustain routine attention but enters full flow states under crisis. The anxious individual who functions normally in low-stakes conditions and fragments under pressure.

These inconsistencies are not noise. They are the signal.

A category that shifts with context is not a category. It is a **state**.

### 3a. The Threshold Architecture: Why ND Traits Are State-Dependent

The deficit model assumes ND differences are structural and fixed. Two 2026 findings directly falsify this framing.

Knudsen et al. (2026) found that autistic individuals show *higher* dopamine D2 receptor availability and *higher* co-localized glucose metabolism in subcortical regions — not lower. The D2 receptor is a gain control mechanism. Higher availability means $I^*$ is hard-wired with high gain on specific channels. This is not a deficit architecture. It is a **high-gain routing architecture running below threshold:**

$$\text{Apparent deficit} = f\left(\frac{A_s^*}{\text{threshold}} < 1\right)$$

$$\text{Strength emergence} = f\left(\frac{A_s^*}{\text{threshold}} \geq 1\right)$$

The fever effect confirms the threshold account: autistic symptoms partially resolve during fever and return when fever breaks. The resolution is not caused by the immune response itself. It is caused by the metabolic upregulation — specifically, the rise in CO₂ and the downstream effects on four simultaneous biological channels: Bohr effect oxygen delivery, cerebral vasodilation, neural firing thresholds, and KCC2/GABAergic function. These channels collectively determine whether $A_s^*$ crosses the routing threshold.

**The critical inference:** you cannot temporarily restore what was never present. If the capacity was absent, fever cannot return it. The capacity is always present. The threshold determines whether it runs.

The parallel to gender is exact. Just as the autistic individual is not deficient but under-threshold, the person exhibiting gendered behavioral traits is not expressing a fixed identity but operating in a particular attractor state. The capacity for a different configuration is present in both cases. The geometry — not the category — determines which one runs.

### 4b. Masking as Pressure-Control Geometry

ND masking and gender performance are structurally identical within the manifold. Neither is an intrinsic trait or an identity-suppression event. Both are **real-time pressure-control mechanisms** designed to maintain social coherence under high external load.

Formally, the total physiological and oscillatory cost of sustained state maintenance is:

$$\text{Masking Cost} = \sum_{i} S_i \cdot C_i$$

Where $S_i$ represents the scaffolded social expectation (monitoring, role enforcement, conformity pressure) and $C_i$ represents the coupling cost — the explicit cross-frequency integration required to override autonomic output and maintain the target attractor.

Hull et al. (2017) established the mechanism for ND masking: it requires continuous explicit monitoring, high executive load, and produces rapid cognitive exhaustion and sensory collapse — the oscillatory signature of $C_i$ expenditure depleting $W$. Raymaker et al. (2020) connected prolonged masking directly to physiological burnout and loss of adaptive function — the endpoint state when masking cost exceeds available width.

The gender performance literature documents the same process under a different label. Performing gender-congruent behaviors under high social monitoring imposes identical dual-task executive costs and elevated sympathetic load — measurable as HRV reduction — as neurodivergent masking.

Under sustained masking, three cascade effects are universal regardless of whether the trigger is ND or gender-related:

1. $W$ narrows — degrees of freedom are dedicated exclusively to constraint enforcement
2. $J$ increases — continuous autonomic strain degrades timing precision
3. $A_h$ shifts toward the conservative attractor

When Masking Cost $> W$, the system undergoes rapid oscillatory collapse. In the ND literature this is called burnout or shutdown. In the gender literature it has no standard term — which is itself evidence that the same process is not being recognized across both domains.

The threshold is the same. The geometry is the same. The difference is the label applied to the collapse.

### 4c. Atypical Lateralization as $A_h$ Geometry: The Left-Handedness Paradox

A long-standing anomaly in neurological and performance literature is the overrepresentation of non-right-handed individuals in two seemingly contradictory domains:

1. High-performance, rapid-adaptation tasks — executive decision-making under uncertainty, elite athletic spatial strategy, complex artistic synthesis
2. Stress-induced motor and sensory disintegration — Functional Neurological Disorder

Standard categorical models treat left-handedness as either a benign trait variation or a subtle developmental impairment. The manifold framework rejects both interpretations. **Handedness is a physical proxy for underlying hemispheric recruitment geometry ($A_h$) and interhemispheric transfer capacity.**

The overrepresentation in both domains is not a paradox. It is the same variable operating in opposite directions under different load conditions.

#### Mechanistic Derivation

In strongly right-handed populations, language and fine motor routing are tightly locked to the left hemisphere, producing a rigid, high-threshold $A_h$ state. In non-right-handed populations, language representation is more bilateral — Knecht et al. (2000) established that left-handers show significantly higher rates of bilateral or right-hemisphere language dominance compared to right-handed controls — and $A_h$ is intrinsically dynamic:

$$\text{Right-handedness} \implies \text{High lateralization lock (fixed } A_h\text{)}$$

$$\text{Left-handedness} \implies \text{High } A_h \text{ variability (dynamic geometry)}$$

Dynamic $A_h$ significantly lowers the computational cost of accessing outer-edge manifold configurations — regions responsible for novel pattern integration, cross-modal processing, and non-linear inference. The same bilateral architecture that makes this access cheaper also means the system lacks a rigid baseline anchor under acute load.

#### The Dual-Outcome Mechanism

$$\text{Performance Outcome} = f(A_h \text{ variability},\; P,\; S)$$

| Condition | Manifold State | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High scaffolding, moderate load | $A_h$ flexibility enables rapid right-hemisphere recruitment without losing left-hemisphere execution | High performance, $M$ attractor — leadership, elite sport, complex synthesis |
| Low/degraded scaffolding, acute stress on chronic baseline | High $P$ and $J$ destabilize $C$ without a rigid $A_h$ anchor | Functional neurological collapse — real symptoms, intact substrate |

This is why the same architectural property — $A_h$ variability — overrepresents left-handers in both high-performance domains and FND risk. The direction of the outcome is determined by scaffolding and load. The underlying variable is the same.

This explains what the research has been unable to parse. Left-handers demonstrating bilateral processing, variable interhemispheric transfer, and unexpected vulnerability under stress have been treated as atypical variance or noise by frameworks assuming fixed lateralization. They are not noise. They are the predicted outputs of a dynamic $A_h$ architecture encountering different load conditions.

#### FND as the Clinical Instantiation

FND affects women at approximately 3:1 over men (Espay et al., 2018), and clinical observation suggests left-handedness is overrepresented in FND populations at rates exceeding the approximately 10% general population baseline. Formal epidemiological confirmation of the handedness signal is pending.

The manifold account predicts both findings and derives them from the same mechanism:

- The gender skew tracks chronic low-power scaffolding narrowing $W$ as a baseline condition — the system is already at Stage 2 when the acute trigger arrives
- The left-handedness signal tracks $A_h$ variability — the same property that makes outer-edge access cheaper also makes rapid configuration shift into collapse geometry more likely when $C$ degrades under load

Both are predicted outputs of one model. Neither requires separate explanation.

#### Prediction and Falsification

The manifold model predicts that:

1. Non-right-handedness will be significantly overrepresented in FND cohorts relative to the 10% general population baseline
2. Left-handed individuals will show greater $A_h$ shift under acute stress compared to strongly lateralized right-handed controls — measurable via task-based EEG asymmetry indexing
3. The same $A_h$ shift that predicts FND vulnerability will positively correlate with outer-edge performance metrics in low-stress, high-scaffolding conditions in the same population

This is falsified if task-based fMRI or EEG demonstrates that $A_h$ dynamics in left-handed individuals under acute stress do not differ significantly from strongly lateralized right-handed controls. The prediction is specific, the methodology is standard, and the measurement exists.

## 7. Falsifiability

The model is falsified if:

- Hemispheric asymmetry does not correlate with pressure across ND and gender populations (falsifies $S \rightarrow A_h$ mechanism)
- Width does not predict behavioral flexibility independent of diagnostic category (falsifies state-dependence claim)
- ND phenotypes do not show oscillatory signatures under load modulation (falsifies geometric account of ND)
- Gendered behavioral traits persist when scaffolding is fully equalized (falsifies power-geometry account)
- HRV geometry does not predict attractor transitions in either population (falsifies manifold as substrate)
- ND and gender-nonconforming populations show *different* collapse trajectories under equivalent load (falsifies shared gradient claim)
- Masking cost is dissociable between ND and gender-performance contexts with different physiological signatures (falsifies structural identity claim in Section 4b)

These are testable with existing methodology: task-based fMRI with dynamic load modulation, real-time HRV under structured pressure conditions, EEG asymmetry indexing under power manipulation, and longitudinal HRV trajectories in resource-stratified samples.
